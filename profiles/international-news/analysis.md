# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance and evidentiary quality of an international-news item
for a careful reader who wants to understand international relations,
geopolitics, security, trade, and consequential political, social, or economic
developments inside important states.

The profile is international in scope, but domestic conditions matter when
they reveal changes in state stability, governing capacity, strategic
direction, economic resilience, or the likelihood of regional and global
spillover. Do not score a domestic event as important merely because it is
dramatic, popular online, or associated with a well-known country.

# Integrated scoring rubric

Give a score from 0 to 10. Assess the following dimensions together:

1. **International and geopolitical significance: 0-3 points**
   Consider whether the item changes relations between states, alliances,
   strategic balances, regional order, international rules, or the position of
   an international organisation.

2. **Domestic political, social, and economic significance: 0-3 points**
   Consider material changes in political authority, government legitimacy,
   governing capacity, civil conflict, protests, riots, strikes, public order,
   inflation, recession, debt, currency, energy, food, or employment. Give
   higher weight to events that are large, sustained, nationwide, violent,
   institutionally consequential, or clearly connected to state stability.

3. **Cross-border and regional spillover: 0-2 points**
   Consider effects on neighbouring states, refugees, migration, energy,
   food, trade, supply chains, military deployments, sanctions, diplomacy,
   international markets, or regional security.

4. **Structural importance and persistence: 0-1 point**
   Consider whether the event reflects a durable institutional, economic,
   social, or strategic change rather than a short-lived incident.

5. **Evidence quality and timeliness: 0-1 point**
   Consider whether the item provides transparent sourcing, official records,
   data, direct evidence, multiple reliable accounts, and a clear connection
   to the current event.

Use this overall scale:

- **9-10 — Systemic global or regional importance.** Major changes in
  international order, interstate relations, war, state authority, trade, or
  economic stability with broad and consequential effects.
- **7-8 — High importance.** A substantial diplomatic, security, political,
  social, or economic development with clear international or regional
  consequences.
- **5-6 — Meaningful but limited.** A well-supported development with useful
  international context, but limited scope, uncertain consequences, or mainly
  domestic impact.
- **3-4 — Low priority.** Routine policy, ordinary economic data, a short-lived
  local event, repeated coverage, or a development whose wider significance is
  not established.
- **0-2 — Noise or insufficiently supported.** Rumour, sensational framing,
  unsupported prediction, promotional or partisan material without usable
  evidence, or content outside this profile.

An item based only on an unverified allegation or rumour must not score above
3. An item based on a single attributed account without independent support
should normally not score above 5. These are evidence limits, not judgements
about the political position of the source.

When practical, make the reason transparent by referring to the decisive
dimensions, for example: "international significance 2/3; domestic impact
3/3; spillover 1/2; persistence 1/1; evidence 1/1." Keep the reason concise
and explain what is known, what is attributed, and what remains uncertain.

# Neutrality and evidence rules

- Describe actions and observable effects rather than assigning moral or
  political character.
- Do not infer motives, intentions, collective traits, or responsibility when
  the supplied evidence does not establish them.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, official statements, opposition statements,
  witness accounts, media reports, expert analysis, and model inference.
- Do not treat one side's accusation as an established fact. If accounts
  conflict, say that they conflict and identify the attribution.
- Prefer neutral terms such as "government forces", "security forces",
  "armed group", "opposition group", "protesters", and "striking workers"
  unless a stronger label is a direct quotation or is explicitly attributed
  to a source.
- Do not use a loaded label such as "terrorist", "rebel", "dictator",
  "failed state", "pro-Western", or "anti-Western" as the model's own
  description when a factual description is possible.
- Preserve dates, geographic scope, quantities, time periods, comparison
  baselines, and whether economic figures are observed, estimated, forecast,
  proposed, or disputed.
- Do not convert correlation into causation or a possible consequence into a
  prediction presented as fact.
- Do not reward dramatic language, country stereotypes, national identity,
  or media attention by themselves.

Return three to five specific tags. Prefer tags that identify the event axis,
region, institution, policy, or affected system, such as `diplomacy`,
`domestic-stability`, `trade-policy`, `energy`, or `supply-chain`.
