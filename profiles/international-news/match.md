# International news

Use this profile when the primary value of an item is understanding an event,
development, or trend that affects relations between countries, regional
stability, international security, global trade, cross-border economic
conditions, or the ability of a state to govern.

This profile includes both international events and important domestic
developments. A domestic event belongs here only when it does at least one of
the following:

- changes a state's political stability, governing capacity, strategic
  direction, or economic condition in a consequential way;
- has a plausible and evidence-supported effect on neighbouring countries,
  migration, energy, food, trade, supply chains, or regional security;
- changes a government's external policy, international commitments, or
  relationship with other states or international organisations;
- reveals a sustained political, social, or economic trend that helps explain
  a broader international development.

Typical items include:

- diplomacy, alliances, elections with international consequences, and
  international organisations;
- wars, military confrontations, ceasefires, armed uprisings, terrorism, and
  security crises;
- coups, attempted coups, civil conflict, separatism, government collapse,
  and major changes in political authority;
- large or sustained protests, riots, strikes, states of emergency, and
  disruptions to public order when their scale or consequences are material;
- inflation, recession, debt, currency, energy, food, or employment crises
  when they affect state stability, foreign policy, or cross-border conditions;
- tariffs, sanctions, export controls, trade agreements, industrial policy,
  and strategic supply-chain changes.

Do not use this profile for routine domestic politics, ordinary market moves,
unverified rumours, investment advice, promotional material, or a local event
with no meaningful connection to international, regional, or state-level
conditions. Do not infer that a country, population, religion, or political
group has a fixed character from one event.

When an item contains competing accounts, classify it by the underlying event
and its evidence, not by which side's description is more forceful. Preserve
the distinction between an event, a claim about the event, and commentary
about its possible meaning.
